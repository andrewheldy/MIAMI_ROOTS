## Purpose

The eight reusable pieces of communication the Founding Connectors program runs on, from
the founder's first message through to a graceful exit. Drafts, ready to use and **pending
an owner voice pass** — the founder should rewrite them until they sound like the founder,
because the entire point of message 1 is that it does not sound automated.

## What belongs here

- The eight message templates, with notes on how to use each

## What does not belong here

- Program rules (see
  [`../01-product/founding-connectors-program.md`](../01-product/founding-connectors-program.md))
- Procedures (see
  [`founding-connectors-operations.md`](founding-connectors-operations.md))

## How to use these

**Placeholders** appear as `{{like_this}}`. Every one of them must be filled — a message
that ships with a placeholder in it does more damage than no message.

**Message 1 is not a template.** It is a shape. Rewrite it per person, in your own words,
with a real specific detail about them in the first two lines. A connector who can tell it
was a mail merge is a connector who will treat the card like junk mail. If you cannot write
a specific first line about someone, that is useful information about whether they belong
in the cohort.

## 1. Personal invitation from the founder

*Sent one to one, by DM or text. Never bulk. Never a link-first message.*

> Hey {{first_name}} — {{specific_true_thing}}. That's the reason I'm writing.
>
> I'm putting together something called Miami Roots. It's a network of the networks that
> already exist here — the run clubs, the studios, the nights, the neighborhood groups —
> so people can actually find each other instead of each scene staying sealed off.
>
> I'm giving cards to about twenty-five people in Miami who are the reason other people
> know each other. You're one of the first I thought of. It's a physical card — someone
> taps their phone on it or scans the code and it opens the door to the community. That's
> the whole thing.
>
> There's no catch and it's not a job. No one's paying anyone per sign-up, and I'm not
> asking you to post anything. You'd just have it on you, and use it when it's genuinely
> the right person.
>
> Can I buy you a coffee and show you? Takes five minutes.
>
> — {{founder_name}}

**Notes.** No link. No pitch deck. No mention of what they get — that comes in person. If
they ask "what's in it for me" before meeting, answer honestly and briefly: early access,
the other connectors, and a say in what this becomes.

## 2. Acceptance message

*Sent when they say yes, before the handoff.*

> That's great — genuinely glad you're in.
>
> You're one of the founding group, which means the shape of this is partly yours. Here's
> what happens next:
>
> **1.** We meet for about five minutes and I hand you your card.
> **2.** I'll show you how it works and we'll test it on your phone.
> **3.** I'll leave you a one-pager with everything on it, so you don't have to remember
> any of it.
>
> Your card has your own code on it: **myroots.dev/r/{{code}}**. That link is yours and it
> doesn't expire.
>
> Two things worth saying up front so there are no surprises: this isn't paid, and there's
> nothing you have to hit. If you use it four times this year and all four people become
> part of the community, that's a great year.
>
> {{time_and_place}} work for you?

## 3. Five-minute onboarding script

*Spoken, at handoff. Not sent. Structured so nothing important gets skipped.*

**(0:00) The card.**
> "So this is yours. Someone taps their phone on it, or scans the code — either one, same
> result. Try it."

*Hand it over. Let them tap it on their own phone. Wait. Do not narrate while they do it.*

**(1:00) What just happened.**
> "That opened Miami Roots. From there it's one tap into the main group chat, which is
> where everything actually happens — events, introductions, people looking for people.
> The link is ours, not WhatsApp's, so if a group link ever changes your card still works.
> It doesn't expire."

**(2:00) What it's for.**
> "It's for the moment you're already having — someone you've just met, or someone you
> know who'd get a lot out of this. Instead of 'I'll send you something', you hand them
> this. That's it."

**(2:45) What it's not for.**
> "It's not for posting everywhere. If it ends up in ten group chats, it stops meaning
> anything — the whole value is that it came from you. Just invite people you'd actually
> vouch for."

**(3:15) What you get.**
> "You're one of the first group, so: you hear about events before anyone, you get in when
> things are capacity-limited, and I'll get the connectors together so you all know each
> other — which honestly is the best part. If you want to be named on the site as a
> founding connector I'll do that, and if you'd rather not, that's the default."

**(4:00) The one thing to ask.**
> "Anything you're unsure about, ask me. Especially if the card ever stops working or you
> lose it — tell me straight away and I'll switch it off and make you a new one, no
> problem."

**(4:30) The recognition question.**
> "Last thing — is it okay if we name you publicly as a founding connector? Totally fine
> either way."

*Record the answer. Default is no.*

**(5:00) End.**
> "That's everything. I'll send you the one-pager so you don't have to remember any of it."

## 4. One-page Founding Connector guide

*Sent as a message or printed. Everything they need, nothing they don't.*

> ### You're a Miami Roots Founding Connector
>
> **Your link:** myroots.dev/r/{{code}}
> **Your card:** tap it or scan it — both go to the same place.
>
> ---
>
> **What Miami Roots is**
> A network of the networks already in Miami. People, events, businesses, opportunities,
> and the local leaders who tie them together. It runs on group chats, real events, and
> introductions that actually happen.
>
> **What you're doing**
> Inviting people you'd genuinely vouch for. That's it. Quality, not quantity — nobody is
> counting.
>
> **How to use the card**
> Hand it over in the moment. Let them tap it. Say one sentence about why you think
> they'd like it. Done.
>
> **What to say**
> Whatever's true for you. If you want a starting point: *"It's a Miami community — chats,
> events, people who actually connect each other. Tap this."*
>
> **Please don't**
> - Post the link in group chats you're not part of, or blast it out
> - Add anyone who hasn't said yes
> - Promise anyone perks, access, work, or money on Miami Roots' behalf
> - Speak or agree to anything on Miami Roots' behalf — you're a connector, not a rep
> - Sell access to the community, or use member info for anything else
>
> **What you get**
> Early invites. Priority when things fill up. Connector-only gatherings. A say in what we
> build. Recognition on the site if you want it — and only if you say so.
>
> **What this isn't**
> A job, a commission, equity, or a permanent arrangement. Nobody's getting paid per
> person. Either side can step away at any time, no hard feelings.
>
> **If something goes wrong**
> Lost the card? Card not working? Someone behaving badly? Message {{founder_contact}}. If
> you lose it I'll switch that card off the same day and make you a new one.
>
> **The community guidelines** apply to you and to everyone you invite:
> myroots.dev/guidelines

## 5. Card handoff checklist

*The admin's checklist, completed before the card leaves your hand. The authoritative copy
is in the operations playbook — this is the pocket version.*

> **Card {{code}} — {{connector_name}} — {{date}}**
>
> - [ ] NFC tap tested on a real phone → opens Miami Roots
> - [ ] QR scans on iPhone (native camera)
> - [ ] QR scans on Android (native camera)
> - [ ] QR scans in poor light
> - [ ] Code on the card matches this connector in the registry
> - [ ] Scan registered in analytics (or noted as not-yet-enabled)
> - [ ] Destination opens and the chat works
> - [ ] No typo — name, code, URL, call to action all read correctly
> - [ ] Tag locked (if supported)
> - [ ] Guidelines / one-pager given to the connector
> - [ ] Public recognition answer recorded: yes / no
>
> Verified by: {{admin_name}}

## 6. One-week follow-up

*Sent about seven days after handoff. The most valuable message in the pack — it catches
friction while it can still be fixed.*

> Hey {{first_name}} — a week in, how's the card doing?
>
> Three things I actually want to know:
>
> **1.** Has it worked every time you've used it? (If it's glitched even once, tell me —
> that's a fixable problem and I'd rather know.)
> **2.** What do you find yourself saying when you hand it over? I keep learning more from
> how you all describe this than from anything I write.
> **3.** Anyone you've brought in who I should know about?
>
> No pressure if the answer is "haven't used it yet" — that's completely normal. Just
> keep it on you.

## 7. Misuse warning

*Sent after a conversation, not instead of one. Direct, unembarrassing, and specific.*

> Hey {{first_name}} — following up on what we talked about.
>
> {{specific_behavior}} isn't something we can keep going with. The reason is simple:
> {{specific_reason_tied_to_the_program}} — and the whole value of the card is that it came
> from a person, to a person.
>
> I'd like to keep you in this. What I'm asking for is {{specific_change}}.
>
> If it carries on, I'll switch the card off — I'd much rather not, so let's just fix it.
> Anything about this you want to push back on, tell me. It's possible I've got the wrong
> end of it.

**Notes.** One behavior, one ask. No lists of grievances. Never send this without having
spoken first, and never send it to a group.

## 8. Graceful removal / deactivation

*Sent when participation ends — whether they chose it, drifted away, or it was our call.
Everyone stays someone we might see at an event next month.*

**If they're stepping back:**

> Totally understood, {{first_name}} — thanks for telling me straight.
>
> I've switched card {{code}} off, so it won't be attributed to you any more. If someone
> taps it, it'll still open the community — I'd rather a stranger get in than hit a dead
> end.
>
> You were one of the first people to carry one of these, and that stays true. You're
> welcome in the community and at the events for as long as you want to be. If you ever
> want to pick it back up, the door's open.

**If it was our decision:**

> {{first_name}} — I've decided to end the connector arrangement, and I wanted to tell you
> myself rather than have you find out by tapping a dead card.
>
> {{one_clear_sentence_of_reason}}.
>
> Card {{code}} is switched off. You're still welcome in the community itself, and the
> guidelines are the same for everyone.
>
> I know this isn't a fun message to get. I'd rather be direct than vague about it.

**Notes.** No group announcement, ever. No explanation to other connectors. If someone
asks, "they're not part of the connector group any more" is a complete answer.
